after: "This is the last tablet of the Baal Cycle, and it covers the poem’s ending. Baal has been swallowed by Mot, Death. The goddess Anat finds his body and buries it on Mount Ṣapan with great sacrifices. El’s court tries to fill the empty throne with ʿAthtar, who turns out to be too small for it. Anat then confronts Mot, splits him with a blade, winnows him, burns him, grinds him and sows him in the field.\n\nIn the passage read here, El dreams a sign that Baal is alive again: the heavens rain oil and the wadis run with honey, and El rejoices. Baal returns, and after seven years Mot comes back to fight him. The two grapple until the sun goddess Shapsh warns Mot that El will overthrow him, and Mot yields. The colophon names the scribe Ilimilku and the chief of the priests."
---

The sixth and last tablet of the Baal Cycle, Ugarit’s long mythological poem about the storm god Baal. It is written in ruled columns of alphabetic cuneiform, and its colophon names the scribe Ilimilku. It was excavated at Ras Shamra and is now in the Louvre.
