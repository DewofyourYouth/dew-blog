after: "Combination I opens as “the account of Balaam son of Beor, a seer of the gods,” the prophet of Numbers 22–24, here outside the Bible.\n\nThe gods come to Balaam at night with a message. In the morning he fasts and weeps, and when his people ask why, he tells them what the gods, the Shaddayin, have decided in their assembly. They have told a goddess, possibly Shamash or Shagar, to close up the heavens with thick cloud and darkness, and to bring silence forever.\n\nWhat follows is a vision of a world turned upside down. Birds behave against their nature: swift and crane revile the eagle, and small birds prey on large ones. Where flocks grazed, the rod is brought. Animals drink wine and heed instruction. The wise are mocked, and the poor woman mixes perfume. The last lines are too broken to follow, but mention Shagar-and-Ashtar, the deaf hearing from afar, and destruction. Combination II, not shown here, is even more fragmentary and seems to concern the underworld."
---

Ink writing in red and black on white plaster, from a building at Tell Deir ʿAlla in the Jordan Valley. It was found in 1967 by a Dutch expedition, broken into more than a hundred fragments, which scholars have grouped into two main “combinations.” Red ink marks headings and certain phrases.

Its language is unusual. Whether it is a form of Aramaic, a Canaanite dialect, or something between the two is still debated.
