after: "The ostracon is a short message, written on both sides, that seems to be from a man away from home to his wife.\n\nOn the front he reports a dream, and a high fever he has had ever since. A vision then appeared to him with the words “health”: he seems to have had the dream interpreted, and the answer was reassuring. On the back he turns practical: if she sells all his valuables, the children will be able to eat, and he adds something about not leaving too little behind."
---

A potsherd written on in ink, from Elephantine, an island in the Nile at Egypt’s southern border where the Persian Empire kept a garrison that included a Jewish military colony. Imperial Aramaic was the administrative language of the empire, and potsherds were the cheap alternative to papyrus for letters and notes.
