after: "The king of Tyre writes to the king of Ugarit, “my brother” in the language of royal diplomacy.\n\nAfter the standard greetings (may the gods guard you; all is well here, send word of how it is with you), he gets to the news. The ships Ugarit had sent to Egypt were caught in a severe storm off Tyre. The ships’ master managed to save the grain in their holds, and the king of Tyre took charge of all of it, along with the crew and their provisions, and then handed it all back. The ships are now moored at Acco, stripped of their rigging. His brother should not worry about it."
---

A small clay tablet written on both faces in Ugaritic alphabetic cuneiform, found at Ras Shamra in the 1950s season among the archives of the royal palace. Ugarit’s palace has produced hundreds of letters, in Ugaritic and in Akkadian, the diplomatic language of the period. Some of its vocabulary, especially the nautical terms, is disputed.
