after: "Mesha, king of Moab, speaks in the first person, and the stele is his account of his whole reign.\n\nHe opens with his name and his father’s, and dedicates the high place at Qarhoh to Kemosh, the god he credits with saving him. Then comes the history: Omri, king of Israel, oppressed Moab for many years because Kemosh was angry with his land, and Omri’s son did the same. In Mesha’s days Kemosh restored the land, and “Israel has perished forever.”\n\nMost of the inscription is a list of campaigns and building works. He takes Ataroth from the men of Gad and kills its people. At Kemosh’s command he takes Nebo from Israel by a night attack, devotes its seven thousand inhabitants to Ashtar-Kemosh, and drags the vessels of YHWH before Kemosh. He drives the king of Israel out of Jahaz. At home he builds Qarhoh’s walls, gates, towers, palace and reservoirs, orders every household to cut its own cistern, builds roads and towns, and resettles the land. The broken last lines begin a campaign against Hauronen, again at Kemosh’s word.\n\nThe same conflict appears from Israel’s side in 2 Kings 3, which ends very differently."
---

A round-topped slab of black basalt, about a metre tall, set up at Dibon (modern Dhiban, Jordan) in the ninth century BCE. Its thirty-four lines make it the longest inscription known from Iron Age Moab, and one of the longest from the whole southern Levant.

Europeans learned of it in 1868. Before it could be removed, local villagers heated it and broke it into pieces. It was reconstructed from the recovered fragments and from a paper squeeze taken before it was smashed, which is why the restored surface looks smoother than the original pieces.
