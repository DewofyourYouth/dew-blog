after: "The section is Mark 1:1–8, the opening of the Gospel. It begins with Isaiah’s prophecy of a messenger and “a voice crying in the wilderness,” and then introduces John the Baptist. John baptizes in the wilderness, proclaiming a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins. All Judea and Jerusalem come out to be baptized in the Jordan, confessing their sins. He wears camel’s hair with a leather belt and eats locusts and wild honey. He announces that someone mightier is coming after him, whose sandal strap he is not worthy to untie: John baptizes with water, and the one to come will baptize with the Holy Spirit.\n\nVerse 4 is the same verse as in the Codex Climaci Rescriptus, so the two dialects can be compared word for word."

The Peshitta is the standard Bible of the Syriac churches, translated into Classical Syriac, the Aramaic dialect of Edessa that became the literary language of Christians across Mesopotamia. The photo shows a ninth-century Peshitta manuscript in the National Library of Israel.
