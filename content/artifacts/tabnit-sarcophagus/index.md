after: "Tabnit gives his titles, priest of Astarte and king of the Sidonians, and his father’s name, Eshmunazar, who held the same titles. Then he addresses whoever finds the coffin: do not open it and do not disturb him, because there is no silver, gold or treasure inside, only him. Opening it is an abomination to Astarte. Whoever does so anyway is cursed with no descendants among the living under the sun and no resting place among the dead.\n\nThat is the whole inscription. The coffin had, of course, already been taken from someone else’s grave."
---

A black stone anthropoid coffin, carved in Egypt for an Egyptian official and later shipped to Sidon. The hieroglyphs on the lid belong to its first owner. A Phoenician inscription of eight lines was added across the foot when it was reused for Tabnit, a king of Sidon.

It was found in 1887 in the royal necropolis of Sidon, during the excavation that also produced the Alexander Sarcophagus, and went to the Imperial Museum in Istanbul.
