after: "The discussion is part of the Yerushalmi’s opening question: when does night begin and day end, for the evening and morning recitation of the Shema.\n\nRabbi Yose son of Rabbi Bun objects that if the dusk at both ends of the day, the time light takes to cross the thickness of the sky, were counted as night, day and night could never be equal. Yet a baraita teaches that at the spring and autumn equinoxes they are equal. Rabbi Huna resolves it with an everyday observation about a king: when he starts to go out, people already say he has gone out, but when he starts to come in, nobody says he has come in until he actually arrives. Night is the same: it “goes out” at the first sign of dawn, but it only “comes in” once it has fully arrived."
---

The Jerusalem Talmud was compiled in the Galilee, in the Aramaic Jews spoke in Israel in late antiquity. The Leiden manuscript, copied in Rome in 1289, is the only complete manuscript of it. It is written in the square script, so the Hebrew letters below are the original letters.
