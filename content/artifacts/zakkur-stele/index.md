after: "Zakkur, king of Hamath and Luʿash, set up the stele for the god Ilwer, and tells his story in the first person.\n\nHe was a humble man whom Baalshamayin made king over Hazrak. Bar-Hadad son of Hazael, king of Aram, then gathered a coalition of seventeen kings, among them the kings of Que, ʿUmq, Gurgum, Samʾal and Meliz, which laid siege to Hazrak with a rampart higher than its walls and a ditch deeper than its ditch. Zakkur lifted his hands to Baalshamayin, who answered through seers and diviners: do not fear, I made you king, I will stand with you and rescue you from all these kings. The end of the front is broken, but the siege was evidently lifted.\n\nThe left side turns to his building works: he rebuilt and enlarged Hazrak, fortified strongholds throughout his territory, and built houses for the gods across his land, and he set up this stele before Ilwer recording what he had done. It closes with a curse on anyone who removes his name or the stele, invoking Baalshamayin, Ilwer, Shamash, Shahar and the gods of heaven and earth. The right side preserves only “the name of Zakkur.”"

A basalt stele of Zakkur, king of Hamath and Luʿash in northern Syria, found in pieces at Tell Afis in 1903 and now in the Louvre. The figure that stood above the text survives only as its feet. It is one of the earliest substantial inscriptions in Aramaic.

The script is the early Aramaic alphabet, close to Phoenician. The lines below are set in Phoenician-block letters, the usual Unicode stand-in for it.
