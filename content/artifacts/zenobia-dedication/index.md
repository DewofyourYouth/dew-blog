after: "The whole inscription is a dedication of a statue of Septimia Batzabbay, known in Greek and Latin as Zenobia, “the illustrious and righteous queen.” Two of her generals, Zabda, the commander-in-chief, and Zabbay, the commander of Palmyra, set it up “for their lady.” It is dated to the month of Ab in the year 582 of the Seleucid era, 271 CE, the year before Aurelian took Palmyra and Zenobia was captured."
---

Palmyra, an oasis city in the Syrian desert, grew rich on the caravan trade and set up hundreds of inscriptions, many of them bilingual in Greek and Palmyrene Aramaic. This one, set on a column, is dated 271 CE, at the height of Palmyra’s brief independence from Rome. It has a Greek counterpart set up beside it.
