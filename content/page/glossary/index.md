Essays here lean on classical rabbinic literature, comparative Semitic linguistics, genealogical history and the tools I use to keep my notes. This page defines the terms that come up most. Where the blog's spelling varies (_Shemos_ and _Shemot_, _Halacha_ and _Halakha_), both forms are given.

## Commentators and Scholars

| Term                         | Period & Region                                           | Definition                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| :--------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Rashi**                    | _1040–1105 (Troyes, France)_                              | Acronym for Rabbi Shlomo Yitzchaki. His line-by-line commentary on Tanach and the Talmud is the standard starting point for traditional study.                                                                |
| **Ibn Ezra**                 | _1089–c. 1167 (Spain, later Italy, France and England)_   | Rabbi Abraham ibn Ezra. Poet, grammarian, astronomer and commentator, known for close attention to Hebrew grammar and the plain sense of the text.                                                            |
| **Rambam**                   | _1138–1204 (Córdoba, later Fustat, Egypt)_                | Acronym for Rabbi Moshe ben Maimon (Maimonides). | **Radak**                    | _c. 1160–1235 (Narbonne, Provence)_                       | Acronym for Rabbi David Kimchi. | **Maharshal**                | _1510–1574 (Poland)_                                      | Rabbi Shlomo Luria, Talmudist and halachic authority.                                                                                                                                                         |

| **Mekubalim**                | _Kabbalists_                                              | Scholars and practitioners of Kabbalah.                                                                                                                                                                       |

| **Shadal**                   | _1800–1865 (Trieste, later Padua)_                        | Shmuel David Luzzatto, scholar, poet, grammarian and Bible commentator; critic of Kabbalah's antiquity.                                                                                                       |

## Texts, Interpretation and Jewish Life

| Term                                              | Origin              | Definition                                                                                                                                         |
| :------------------------------------------------ | :------------------ | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Tanach**                                        | Hebrew acronym      | The Hebrew Bible: Torah (the Five Books), Nevi'im (Prophets) and Ketuvim (Writings).                                                               |
| **Chumash**                                       | Hebrew              | The Five Books of Moses in printed book form, usually with commentaries.                                                                           |
| **Bereishis, Shemos, Vayikra, Bamidbar, Devarim** | Hebrew              | The Hebrew names of Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers and Deuteronomy (also spelled _Bereshit_, _Shemot_).                                       |
| **Tehillim**                                      | Hebrew              | The book of Psalms.                                                                                                                                |
| **Pasuk (pl. pesukim)**                           | Hebrew              | A verse of Tanach.                                                                                                                                 |
| **Parsha / Parashah**                             | Hebrew              | The weekly Torah portion read in synagogue.                                                                                                        |
| **Mishnah**                                       | Hebrew              | The first written code of the Oral Torah, edited around 200 CE by Rabbi Yehudah HaNasi and arranged in tractates.                                  |
| **Talmud**                                        | Hebrew              | The Mishnah together with the Gemara, the rabbis' extended discussion of it. The Babylonian Talmud is the one usually meant.                       |
| **Tractate (masechta)**                           | Aramaic             | One volume of the Mishnah or Talmud on a single subject, such as _Berachos_ (blessings and prayer) or _Menachos_ (grain offerings).                |
| **Zevachim**                                      | Hebrew              | The tractate on animal offerings. Its final chapter discusses when sacrifice outside the Temple, on a _bamah_, was permitted.                      |
| **Perek**                                         | Hebrew              | A chapter of the Mishnah, Talmud or Tanach.                                                                                                        |
| **Sugya**                                         | Aramaic             | A self-contained passage or debate within the Talmud.                                                                                              |
| **Shulchan Aruch**                                | Hebrew              | The code of Jewish law by Rabbi Yosef Karo (1565), with the Rema's Ashkenazi glosses.                                                              |
| **Siddur**                                        | Hebrew              | The Jewish prayer book.                                                                                                                            |
| **Pesukei DeZimra**                               | Aramaic             | "Verses of song": the psalms and praises said before the core of the morning service.                                                              |
| **Korbanot**                                      | Hebrew              | Passages about the Temple offerings, recited at the start of the morning service.                                                                  |
| **Peshat**                                        | Hebrew              | The plain, contextual meaning of a text.                                                                                                           |
| **Drash / Derash**                                | Hebrew              | Interpretation beyond the plain meaning: homiletic, legal or allegorical.                                                                          |
| **Midrash**                                       | Hebrew              | Rabbinic literature that interprets biblical texts through exposition, narrative and parable.                                                      |
| **Zohar**                                         | Aramaic             | The central work of Kabbalah, a mystical commentary on the Torah that surfaced in 13th-century Spain; its antiquity is disputed.                   |
| **Shema**                                         | Hebrew              | The declaration "Hear, O Israel" (Deuteronomy 6:4–9), recited every morning and evening.                                                           |
| **Sefer**                                         | Hebrew              | A book, especially a work of Torah literature.                                                                                                     |
| **Teshuva (responsum)**                           | Hebrew              | A rabbi's written answer to a question of Jewish law; collections of them are _responsa_.                                                          |
| **Dikduk**                                        | Hebrew              | Hebrew grammar: vowels, verb patterns (_binyanim_) and roots.                                                                                      |
| **Halacha / Halakha**                             | Hebrew              | Jewish law, derived from the Written and Oral Torah.                                                                                               |
| **Minhag**                                        | Hebrew              | Custom: an established communal practice, which carries its own weight in Jewish law.                                                              |
| **Middat chasidut**                               | Hebrew              | A measure of piety: conduct beyond what the law requires, praised but not obligatory.                                                              |
| **Yotzei**                                        | Hebrew              | Having fulfilled an obligation. To be _yotzei_ by every opinion is to satisfy all of them.                                                         |
| **Avodah**                                        | Hebrew              | Divine service: prayer and religious practice understood as serving God.                                                                           |
| **Kavana**                                        | Hebrew              | Concentration and intention, especially in prayer.                                                                                                 |
| **Tzitzit**                                       | Hebrew              | The fringes the Torah requires on the corners of a four-cornered garment (Numbers 15:38).                                                          |
| **Kabbalah**                                      | Hebrew              | The Jewish mystical tradition.                                                                                                                     |
| **Hasidism (Chassidus)**                          | Hebrew              | The pietist movement founded in 18th-century Ukraine by the Baal Shem Tov, organized around dynasties of rebbes.                                   |
| **Hashem**                                        | Hebrew              | Literally "the Name": a reverent way of referring to God.                                                                                          |
| **Shabbos / Shabbat**                             | Hebrew              | The Sabbath, from Friday sunset to Saturday nightfall.                                                                                             |
| **Daven / davening**                              | Yiddish             | To pray, especially the set daily prayers.                                                                                                         |
| **Shul**                                          | Yiddish             | A synagogue.                                                                                                                                       |
| **Tefillin**                                      | Hebrew              | Leather boxes holding Torah passages, worn on the arm and head during weekday morning prayers.                                                     |
| **Mussar**                                        | Hebrew              | Jewish ethical literature, and the practice of deliberately working on one's character.                                                            |
| **Teshuvah**                                      | Hebrew              | Repentance; literally "return."                                                                                                                    |
| **Yeshiva**                                       | Hebrew              | An academy for advanced Torah and Talmud study.                                                                                                    |
| **Kollel**                                        | Hebrew              | A Talmud academy for married men, usually with a stipend.                                                                                          |
| **Shiur**                                         | Hebrew              | A Torah lecture or class.                                                                                                                          |
| **Chavrusa**                                      | Aramaic             | A study partner; Talmud is traditionally learned in pairs.                                                                                         |
| **Litvak**                                        | Yiddish             | A Lithuanian Jew, and by extension the analytical yeshiva tradition that came out of Lithuania.                                                    |
| **Bracha (pl. brachos)**                          | Hebrew              | A blessing, such as the one said before eating bread (_hamotzi_).                                                                                  |
| **Elul**                                          | Hebrew              | The month before Rosh Hashanah, set aside for introspection and repentance.                                                                        |
| **Yahrzeit**                                      | Yiddish             | The anniversary of a death, kept on its Hebrew calendar date.                                                                                      |
| **Tzidkat HaTzaddik**                             | Hebrew              | "The Righteousness of the Righteous": Rav Tzadok's book of short numbered teachings on spiritual life.                                             |
| **Os (pl. otiyot)**                               | Hebrew              | Literally "letter": a numbered paragraph in a book like _Tzidkat HaTzaddik_.                                                                       |
| **Yisrael**                                       | Hebrew              | Israel: the name Yaakov received after wrestling the angel (Genesis 32:29), and so the name of his descendants.                                    |
| **Shevatim**                                      | Hebrew              | The tribes of Israel, each descended from one of Yaakov's sons.                                                                                    |
| **Moshe Rabbeinu**                                | Hebrew              | "Moshe our teacher": Moses, through whom the Torah was given.                                                                                      |
| **Kohen (pl. kohanim)**                           | Hebrew              | A priest: a male descendant of Aharon, Moshe's brother and the first high priest.                                                                  |
| **Maalah (pl. maalot)**                           | Hebrew              | A spiritual quality or attainment; literally "a step up."                                                                                          |
| **Av batorah**                                    | Hebrew              | A father in Torah: a teacher, counted as a parent to the students he teaches.                                                                      |
| **Hergel and toldah**                             | Hebrew              | Habit and birth: what a person acquires by living around someone, as opposed to what he is born with.                                              |
| **Tzaddik**                                       | Hebrew              | A righteous person; tradition calls Yosef _the_ tzaddik.                                                                                           |
| **Chassid**                                       | Hebrew              | A pious person who goes beyond what the law requires.                                                                                              |
| **Kedusha**                                       | Hebrew              | Holiness: being set apart for God.                                                                                                                 |
| **Tahara**                                        | Hebrew              | Ritual purity.                                                                                                                                     |
| **Middot (sing. middah)**                         | Hebrew              | Character traits; also the particular spiritual bent of a person or group.                                                                         |
| **Chochma**                                       | Hebrew              | Wisdom.                                                                                                                                            |
| **Talmid chacham (pl. talmidei chachamim)**       | Hebrew              | Literally "a student of a wise man": a Torah scholar.                                                                                              |
| **Ger (pl. gerim)**                               | Hebrew              | A convert to Judaism.                                                                                                                              |
| **Zera Yisrael**                                  | Hebrew              | "Seed of Israel": someone of Jewish descent, typically through the father, who is not Jewish by halacha.                                           |
| **Kinui**                                         | Hebrew              | A by-name or epithet: what someone is called, as distinct from what he is.                                                                         |
| **Avodah zarah**                                  | Hebrew              | Literally "strange worship": idolatry.                                                                                                             |
| **Oven of Achnai**                                | Aramaic             | The Talmud's dispute (Bava Metzia 59b) in which the sages overrule Rabbi Eliezer despite his miracles and a heavenly voice: "It is not in heaven." |
| **Derech Hashem**                                 | Hebrew              | "The Way of God": Ramchal's systematic account of God, providence and the purpose of creation.                                                     |
| **Mesillat Yesharim**                             | Hebrew              | "The Path of the Just": Ramchal's mussar classic, climbing step by step from watchfulness to holiness.                                             |
| **Cheshbon**                                      | Hebrew              | An account or reckoning: the reasoning by which someone justifies what he does.                                                                    |
| **Mesirat nefesh**                                | Hebrew              | Literally "handing over the soul": self-sacrifice, from giving up what you want to giving up your life.                                            |
| **Nefesh**                                        | Hebrew              | Soul, life, self; also what drives a person.                                                                                                       |
| **Tachanun**                                      | Hebrew              | Supplications said after the Amidah on most weekdays.                                                                                              |
| **Nefilat apayim**                                | Hebrew              | "Falling on the face": resting one's head on the arm during Tachanun.                                                                              |
| **Mitzvah (pl. mitzvot)**                         | Hebrew              | A commandment; loosely, a good deed.                                                                                                               |
| **Mezuzah**                                       | Hebrew              | A parchment of Torah verses fixed to a doorpost (Deuteronomy 6:9).                                                                                 |
| **Techelet**                                      | Hebrew              | The blue dye the Torah requires for one thread of the tzitzit (Numbers 15:38).                                                                     |
| **Rashei sanhedraot**                             | Hebrew              | Heads of Sanhedrins: leaders of the high courts of Torah law.                                                                                      |
| **Satan**                                         | Hebrew              | The accuser or tempter; in Jewish thought he acts within God's will rather than as an independent power.                                           |
| **Chutzpah**                                      | Hebrew, via Yiddish | Audacity, nerve.                                                                                                                                   |
| **Schlep**                                        | Yiddish             | A tiresome trek or chore; to drag something along.                                                                                                 |
| **Torah**                                         | Hebrew              | The Five Books of Moses; more broadly, the whole body of Jewish teaching, written and oral.                                                        |
| **Adar**                                          | Hebrew calendar     | The month that falls around February–March, near the end of the rainy season in the Land of Israel.                                                |
| **Sephardi**                                      | Hebrew              | Of the Jews of Spanish and Portuguese descent; loosely, of Middle Eastern and North African Jewry as well.                                         |
| **Ashkenazi**                                     | Hebrew              | Of the Jews of Central and Eastern European descent.                                                                                               |
| **Targum (pl. targumim)**                         | Aramaic             | An Aramaic translation of the Bible, such as Targum Onkelos on the Torah.                                                                          |
| **Talmud Bavli**                                  | Aramaic             | The Babylonian Talmud, compiled in the academies of Babylonia (c. 500 CE): the Talmud usually studied.                                             |
| **Yerushalmi**                                    | Hebrew              | The Jerusalem Talmud, compiled in the Land of Israel (c. 400 CE) in a western Aramaic dialect.                                                     |
| **Ketav Ivri**                                    | Hebrew              | The Paleo-Hebrew script: the older Hebrew alphabet, close to Phoenician, used before the square script.                                            |
| **Mishloach manot**                               | Hebrew              | Gifts of food sent to friends on Purim.                                                                                                            |
| **Tannaim (sing. tanna)**                         | Aramaic             | The sages of the Mishnah (c. 10–220 CE).                                                                                                           |
| **Ruach hakodesh**                                | Hebrew              | The holy spirit: divine inspiration short of full prophecy.                                                                                        |
| **Kippah**                                        | Hebrew              | The skullcap worn by observant Jewish men.                                                                                                         |
| **Breslover**                                     | Yiddish             | A follower of Rabbi Nachman of Breslov.                                                                                                            |
| **Aveira (pl. aveirot)**                          | Hebrew              | A sin, a transgression.                                                                                                                            |
| **Vikuach al Chokhmat HaKabbalah**                | Hebrew              | Shadal's "Dialogue on the Wisdom of Kabbalah" (1852), arguing that the Zohar is a late composition.                                                |
| **Likkutei Halakhot**                             | Hebrew              | Rav Natan's multi-volume work reading the laws of the _Shulchan Aruch_ through Rabbi Nachman's teachings.                                          |
| **Ta'amim**                                       | Hebrew              | The cantillation marks written with the biblical text, governing its chant and phrasing.                                                           |
| **Kotzk**                                         | Hasidic court       | The court of Rabbi Menachem Mendel of Kotzk (1787–1859), famous for its uncompromising demand for truth.                                           |
| **Chabad**                                        | Hasidic movement    | Founded by Rabbi Shneur Zalman of Liadi (1745–1812), known for its intellectual system of Hasidic thought.                                         |
| **Madrega (pl. madregot)**                        | Hebrew              | A spiritual level or rung.                                                                                                                         |
| **Elokut**                                        | Hebrew              | Godliness: the divine, as an object of contemplation in Hasidic thought.                                                                           |
| **Karkafta delo manach tefillin**                 | Aramaic             | "A skull that has not put on tefillin": the Talmud's term (Rosh Hashanah 17a) for a Jew who never wore them.                                       |
| **Keli (pl. kelim)**                              | Hebrew              | A vessel; in Hasidic and kabbalistic thought, the container through which divine flow can be received.                                             |
| **Shefa**                                         | Hebrew              | Abundance: the flow of divine blessing into the world.                                                                                             |
| **Bakashot**                                      | Hebrew              | Requests, petitions; the middle blessings of the Amidah.                                                                                           |
| **Refa'einu**                                     | Hebrew              | "Heal us": the Amidah's blessing for the sick.                                                                                                     |
| **Tallit**                                        | Hebrew              | The fringed prayer shawl worn during morning prayers.                                                                                              |
| **Shacharit**                                     | Hebrew              | The morning prayer service.                                                                                                                        |
| **Rosh Hashanah**                                 | Hebrew              | The Jewish New Year, on which, the Mishnah says, the world is judged.                                                                              |
| **Yom Kippur**                                    | Hebrew              | The Day of Atonement, ten days after Rosh Hashanah, when the year's judgment is sealed.                                                            |
| **Gan Eden**                                      | Hebrew              | The Garden of Eden; also the realm where souls are rewarded after death.                                                                           |
| **Gehinnom**                                      | Hebrew              | The place or process of punishment after death, usually understood as purifying and temporary.                                                     |
| **Agmat nefesh**                                  | Hebrew              | Anguish; literally "grief of the soul."                                                                                                            |
| **Yerida letzorech aliya**                        | Hebrew              | "Descent for the sake of ascent": a fall that makes a greater rise possible.                                                                       |
| **Mashal (pl. meshalim)**                         | Hebrew              | A parable told to illustrate a point.                                                                                                              |
| **Baalei mussar**                                 | Hebrew              | Masters of _mussar_: teachers and practitioners of deliberate work on character.                                                                   |

## Semitic Languages and Scripts

| Term                                | Domain                  | Definition                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| :---------------------------------- | :---------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Semitic languages**               | Language family         | The family that includes Hebrew, Arabic, Aramaic, Amharic and Ge'ez, as well as ancient languages such as Akkadian, Ugaritic and Phoenician.                                                                                                                              |
| **Proto-Semitic**                   | Linguistics             | The reconstructed common ancestor of the Semitic languages. It is not attested in writing.                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **Canaanite languages**             | Northwest Semitic       | The branch that includes Hebrew, Phoenician, Moabite, Ammonite and Edomite.                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| **Canaanite shift**                 | Historical phonology    | The sound change by which an inherited long _ā_ became _ō_ in the Canaanite languages, as in Arabic _salām_ and Hebrew _shalom_.                                                                                                                                          |
| **Phoenician**                      | Northwest Semitic       | The Canaanite language of Tyre, Sidon and Byblos. Its alphabet is the ancestor of the Hebrew, Aramaic and Greek scripts.                                                                                                                                                  |
| **Moabite**                         | Northwest Semitic       | The Canaanite language of Moab, east of the Dead Sea, known mainly from the Mesha Stele (9th century BCE).                                                                                                                                                                |
| **Kemosh**                          | Moabite religion        | The national god of Moab, named in Numbers 21:29 and credited by King Mesha with his victories.                                                                                                                                                                           |
| **Mishnaic Hebrew**                 | Hebrew                  | The Hebrew of the Mishnah and early rabbinic literature, distinct in vocabulary and grammar from Biblical Hebrew.                                                                                                                                                         |
| **Aramaic**                         | Northwest Semitic       | A Northwest Semitic language on a separate branch from Hebrew (which is Canaanite), and the lingua franca of the region from the Persian period. Centuries of contact shaped Mishnaic and later Hebrew. Parts of Daniel and Ezra, and most of the Talmud, are in Aramaic. |
| **Ugaritic**                        | Northwest Semitic       | An extinct language written in an alphabetic cuneiform on clay tablets from Ugarit (northern Syria, c. 14th–12th century BCE); important for comparing biblical Hebrew vocabulary.                                                                                        |
| **Safaitic**                        | Ancient North Arabian   | A script, and the early form of Arabic written in it, known from thousands of rock inscriptions left by nomads in the basalt desert of southern Syria, northeastern Jordan and northern Saudi Arabia (c. 1st century BCE–4th century CE).                                 |
| **Epigraphy**                       | Archaeology             | The study of inscriptions on durable surfaces such as stone, rock and clay.                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| **Abjad**                           | Writing systems         | A script whose letters record consonants, leaving most vowels to the reader. Hebrew, Aramaic, Arabic and Phoenician are abjads.                                                                                                                                           |
| **Matres lectionis (imos hakriah)** | Writing systems         | "Mothers of reading": consonant letters, chiefly ו and י, used to mark vowels. How often they are written is a matter of spelling convention.                                                                                                                             |
| **Nikud**                           | Hebrew                  | The vowel points written around Hebrew letters.                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
